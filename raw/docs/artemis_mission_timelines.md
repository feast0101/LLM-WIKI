# Artemis Space Program: Mission Timelines

*Source: NASA Artemis II Reference Guide (January 2025)*

---

## Overview

The Artemis program is a long-term exploration campaign designed to establish a permanent human presence on the Moon and serve as a foundation for future crewed missions to Mars. It involves a series of increasingly complex missions building toward sustained lunar presence and eventually human exploration of Mars.

---

## Artemis I — Uncrewed Test Flight (Completed)

**Duration:** ~25.5 days
**Launch:** November 16, 2022
**Splashdown:** December 11, 2022

### Key Events
- **Launch:** First integrated flight of the Space Launch System (SLS) rocket and Orion spacecraft
- **Lunar Orbit:** Orion completed two lunar flybys, coming within 80 miles of the surface, and entered a distant retrograde orbit
- **Record Distance:** Traveled 1.4 million miles; reached a maximum distance of **268,563 miles from Earth** — farther than any spacecraft built for humans has ever flown
- **Re-entry & Recovery:** Orion performed a skip-entry technique before splashing down in the Pacific Ocean west of Baja California

## Artemis II — First Crewed Mission

**Duration:** ~10 days | **Total Distance:** 685,000+ miles
**Crew:** 4 astronauts
**Launch Site:** Launch Pad 39B, Kennedy Space Center, Florida

### Phase 1: Launch and Ascent (Day 1)

| Mission Elapsed Time | Event |
|---|---|
| T+00:00:00 | Liftoff |
| ~T+00:00:07 | Tower clear; roll/pitch maneuver begins |
| ~T+00:00:55 | Engines throttle down approaching Max Q |
| ~T+00:01:11 | **Max Q** — maximum atmospheric stress |
| ~T+00:02:08 | **Solid Rocket Booster (SRB) Separation** at ~148,384 ft altitude |
| ~T+00:03:28 | **Launch Abort System (LAS) Jettison** |
| ~T+00:08:06 | **Core Stage Main Engine Cut Off (MECO)** at 511,000+ ft altitude |
| ~T+00:08:18 | **Core Stage / ICPS Separation** |
| ~T+00:49:52 | ICPS **Perigee Raise Maneuver** |

### Phase 2: High Earth Orbit and Proximity Operations (Days 1–2)

Orion performs two elliptical orbits around Earth to validate all systems before committing to the lunar transit. A 23.5-hour checkout period follows the apogee raise burn.

| Mission Elapsed Time | Event |
|---|---|
| ~T+01:48:48 | ICPS **Apogee Raise Burn** → enters high Earth orbit |
| ~T+03:25:41 | **Proximity Operations Demonstration begins** — crew manually pilots Orion (~2 hrs) using ICPS as visual target to simulate docking |
| ~T+04:53:36 | Proximity Operations end; crew checks environmental control and life support systems |
| Post-separation | ICPS performs disposal burn; deploys 5 CubeSats (ATENEA, TACHELES, Space Weather CubeSat-1, K-Rad Cube); re-enters over Pacific |

### Phase 3: Trans-Lunar Injection and Outbound Transit (Days 2–5)

- **TLI Burn:** Orion's main engine performs the Trans-Lunar Injection burn to leave Earth orbit and begin the lunar free-return trajectory
- **Transit Duration:** ~4 days to reach the Moon
- **En route operations:** Outbound Trajectory Correction (OTC) burns as needed
- **Daily crew routine:** 30 min exercise (flywheel device), 3 meals (1 shared 60-min mealtime), 8 hrs synchronized sleep

### Phase 4: Lunar Flyby (Day 5–6)

- Orion travels **~4,700 miles beyond the far side of the Moon**
- Mean altitude during far-side flyby: ~4,047 miles
- Crew documents the lunar surface via photographs and audio recordings
- First humans to see the Moon up close since 1972

### Phase 5: Return Transit (Days 6–10)

- Gravity pulls Orion back toward Earth (~4-day transit)
- Crew performs Return Trajectory Correction (RTC) burns as needed to target the precise atmospheric entry point

### Phase 6: Re-entry and Recovery (Day 10)

| Event | Detail |
|---|---|
| Crew Module / Service Module separation | Before entry interface |
| **Entry Interface** | Re-entry at **30× speed of sound** (~25,000 mph) |
| ~23,000 ft altitude | Forward bay cover jettisoned; parachute sequence begins |
| Parachute deployment | 11 parachutes slow capsule from 325 mph to ≤20 mph |
| **Splashdown** | Pacific Ocean, off San Diego coast |
| Stabilization | Crew Module Uprighting System deploys 5 airbags |
| Crew extraction | Navy divers install stabilization collar; astronauts extracted one by one via helicopter |
| Recovery goal | Crew aboard recovery ship **within 2 hours** of splashdown |

> **First time:** open-water extraction of crew while capsule remains in the ocean.

## Artemis III — First Lunar Surface Landing

**Goal:** Land the first humans at the **Moon's South Pole region**

- Crew of four travel to the Moon to establish the first human footprint at the lunar South Pole
- Artemis II flight maneuvers and operations serve as direct practice for this mission
- Specific mission elapsed timeline to be published closer to launch

---

## Future Missions — Sustained Lunar Presence

### Capabilities Introduced in Later Missions
- **Exploration Upper Stage (EUS):** More powerful upper stage replacing the ICPS used on Artemis I and II
- **Rendezvous and Docking System:** To interface with human landing systems and other spacecraft
- **Gateway:** Lunar-orbiting outpost developed with international and commercial partners

### Long-Term Goals
- Establish a long-term, permanent foothold in deep space
- Develop technologies and living protocols at the Moon to support future human missions to **Mars**
