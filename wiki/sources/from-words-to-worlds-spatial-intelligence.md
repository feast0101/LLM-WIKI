# From Words to Worlds: Spatial Intelligence is AI's Next Frontier

## Summary

Fei-Fei Li's essay argues that while large language models have transformed how we access abstract knowledge, they remain fundamentally limited: "eloquent but inexperienced, knowledgeable but ungrounded." The next frontier in AI is spatial intelligence—the ability to understand, reason about, and interact with the physical and virtual worlds in three dimensions.

The essay positions spatial intelligence as the missing link in AI's evolution. Language models excel at processing symbolic, abstract concepts but lack grounding in physical reality. Spatial intelligence, by contrast, requires understanding how things look from different angles, how they move and change, how they interact under physical laws, and how to manipulate them.

The author traces the vision to Turing's 1950 question ("Can machines think?") and argues that the field has spent decades on language and logic but neglected the spatial foundation that embodied cognition requires. She emphasizes that this shift is not incremental; it represents a fundamental reorientation of AI research from abstract symbol manipulation toward sensorimotor understanding of the physical world.

The piece discusses how spatial intelligence will revolutionize multiple domains: storytelling and media creation, robotics and embodied agents, scientific discovery and simulation, and the design of virtual and augmented reality systems. The essay argues that spatial intelligence is not just another AI capability but a prerequisite for artificial general intelligence.
