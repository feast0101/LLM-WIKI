# Neural Network

## Definition

A neural network is the interconnected system of neurons in the brain that transmit information via synaptic connections. The brain contains approximately 100 billion neurons arranged in complex networks with trillions of synaptic connections. Unlike static circuits, neural networks are dynamically changeable through neuroplasticity.

## Why It Matters

Neural networks are the basis of all brain function and cognition. Understanding how neural networks organize themselves, encode information, and change with learning is fundamental to neuroscience and brain-machine interfaces.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Local circuits**: Small networks of neurons within specific brain regions.
- **Distributed networks**: Large-scale networks spanning multiple brain regions connected via white matter tracts.
- **Feed-forward networks**: Information flows from input to output without recurrent connections.
- **Recurrent networks**: Neurons connect back to themselves or earlier stages, allowing complex dynamics and memory.

## Evidence

- Neurons are similar to computer transistors in using binary information (action potential or no action potential), but unlike transistors, neurons are constantly changing their connections and strengths. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- The brain contains approximately 100 billion neurons, with around 15-20 billion in the cortex; the remaining neurons are in the cerebellum and brainstem. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- Learning involves lasting structural changes to neural connections, including shifts in neuron shape and location and strengthening or weakening of synaptic connections. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
