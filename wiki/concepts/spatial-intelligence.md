# Spatial Intelligence

## Definition

Spatial intelligence is the capacity of AI systems to understand, reason about, and interact with the physical and virtual worlds in three dimensions. It encompasses the ability to perceive 3D structure, understand how objects move and interact under physical laws, and manipulate or navigate spatial environments. Spatial intelligence contrasts fundamentally with symbolic reasoning or text processing—it is grounded in understanding how the world actually works.

## Why It Matters

Spatial intelligence is positioned as the next frontier in AI research, following the era of language model dominance. Large language models excel at abstract symbolic knowledge but remain "wordsmiths in the dark"—eloquent but ungrounded in physical reality. Spatial intelligence is essential for artificial systems to understand, imagine, reason about, and interact with the real world. It is foundational for robotics, autonomous vehicles, scientific discovery, and eventually artificial general intelligence. The shift from language to spatial understanding represents a fundamental reorientation of AI toward embodied cognition.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Perception-based** — Understanding 3D structure from images or point clouds
- **Physics-based** — Reasoning about object dynamics and physical interactions
- **Action-grounded** — Coupling perception with motor control and embodied learning
- **Simulation-based** — Learning spatial reasoning through interaction in simulated environments
