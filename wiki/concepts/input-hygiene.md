# Input Hygiene

## Definition

Input hygiene refers to the practice of preparing the mind for deep learning by first skimming or surveying material to create a conceptual framework. This initial, low-effort pass involves reading headings, looking at diagrams, reading summaries, and reviewing end-of-chapter questions—without attempting to deeply understand the material. This primes the brain with a rough mental map before engaging in focused, deep study.

## Why It Matters

Input hygiene leverages the brain's capacity for chunking and top-down understanding. By first understanding the overall structure and key concepts, the subsequent focused learning is more efficient and effective. Without input hygiene, readers may get lost in details without understanding how they fit into the larger picture.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Skim first**: Read headings, summaries, and questions before deep reading.
- **Diagram preview**: Examine figures, charts, and visual summaries before reading text.
- **Learning objectives**: Review what you're supposed to learn before attempting to learn it.

## Evidence

- Before studying a chapter or topic, do a quick skim: headings, diagrams, summary, end-of-chapter questions to "prime" your brain for what matters. [[sources/learning-how-to-learn]]
- This initial pass helps your focused work later because your brain already has a rough map of what you're about to learn. [[sources/learning-how-to-learn]]
