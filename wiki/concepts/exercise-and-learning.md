# Exercise and Learning

## Definition

Physical exercise is a lifestyle factor that enhances cognitive function and learning effectiveness. Exercise increases blood flow to the brain, promotes the release of neurotrophic factors (proteins that support neural growth), reduces stress hormones, and improves executive function and attention. Regular physical activity is one of the most evidence-based interventions for improving learning performance.

## Why It Matters

Exercise is a readily accessible, free intervention that improves learning outcomes across all age groups. The connection between physical fitness and cognitive function is so strong that exercise should be considered a core component of any learning strategy, alongside focused work and sleep.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Aerobic exercise**: Running, cycling, swimming; most strongly linked to cognitive benefits.
- **Resistance training**: Weight lifting; also supports cognitive function and neuroplasticity.
- **Timing**: Exercise earlier in the day or before studying is most beneficial for learning.

## Evidence

- Regular physical exercise improves learning effectiveness. [[sources/learning-how-to-learn]]
- Tackling your hardest or most disliked material earlier in the day—when energy and willpower are highest—improves learning effectiveness. [[sources/learning-how-to-learn]]
