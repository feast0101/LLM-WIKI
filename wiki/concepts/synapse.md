# Synapse

## Definition

A synapse is the junction between two neurons where an axon terminal of one neuron meets the dendrite of another neuron. Synapses are the sites of information transfer, typically mediated by chemical neurotransmitters released from the presynaptic terminal and received by receptors on the postsynaptic membrane.

## Why It Matters

Synapses are the fundamental connection points in neural networks and are where learning occurs through changes in synaptic strength. Understanding synaptic function is critical to understanding how neural circuits compute and how brain-machine interfaces can decode complex neural information.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Chemical synapses**: The majority type; transmit information via neurotransmitter molecules.
- **Electrical synapses**: Direct connections where the presynaptic and postsynaptic terminals are electrically coupled; allow rapid signal transmission in time-critical situations.

## Evidence

- Synapses are the points of contact where axon terminals of one neuron touch the dendrites of other neurons. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- When an action potential reaches an axon terminal, it triggers the release of neurotransmitter chemicals into the synapse, which may trigger action potentials in the downstream neuron. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- Synaptic transmission involves a tiny gap between neurons; information is conveyed by chemical molecules crossing this gap. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
