# Social Law (Governing-Law Regime)

## Definition

One of four governing-law regimes in the agentic world modeling taxonomy. The Social Law regime encompasses systems where world models must respect agent beliefs, goals, norms, and coordination constraints. Domains include multi-agent settings, social simulation, dialogue, and collaborative reasoning. Social laws differ from physical and digital laws in that they emerge from agent intentions, social structures, and conventional norms rather than physical or computational constraints.

## Why It Matters

Social-law world models enable multi-agent reasoning, negotiation, and coordination. An agent must model not just the physical environment but also the beliefs, goals, and strategies of other agents. Social worlds have fewer absolute constraints than physical or digital worlds—agents can violate social norms—but violations have predictable consequences (shame, retaliation, ostracism). Understanding social-law constraints is essential for agents operating in human-AI collaboration, multi-agent teams, and social simulation systems.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Belief modeling**: Represent other agents' mental states (beliefs, goals, intentions)
- **Goal inference**: Infer what other agents are trying to accomplish from their actions
- **Norm-respecting simulation**: Model social norms and predict how agents respond to norm violations
- **Dialogue and language grounding**: Simulate dialogue dynamics and language understanding
- **Reputation and trust modeling**: Track how agents' actions affect social relationships
- **Game-theoretic reasoning**: Predict strategic behavior in multi-agent games

## Open Questions

- What role do norms play in constraining agent behavior, and how are they learned?
- Can social-law simulators handle deception, irrationality, and bounded rationality in agent models?
- How do social-law world models handle the open-endedness of human social behavior?
- How are social-law simulators evaluated: by prediction accuracy, task success, or human judgment?
