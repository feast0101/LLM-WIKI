# Spaced Repetition

## Definition

Spaced repetition is a learning technique in which material is reviewed at increasing intervals over days, weeks, or months rather than massed together in a single session (cramming). Each review is timed just before the learner would have forgotten the material, forcing retrieval and reconsolidation at progressively longer delays.

## Why It Matters

Spaced repetition leverages the spacing effect—a fundamental finding in cognitive psychology showing that distributed practice produces stronger, more durable memories than massed practice. By spacing review sessions optimally, spaced repetition creates robust memories that resist forgetting and can be flexibly applied to new problems.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Fixed spacing**: Reviewing material at predetermined intervals (e.g., every 1 day, 3 days, 1 week).
- **Adaptive spacing**: Adjusting intervals based on performance; spacing longer for material that is well-learned and shorter for difficult material.
- **Expanding intervals**: Starting with short delays (1 day) and progressively increasing (3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks) as mastery improves.

## Evidence

- Revisiting material over several days or weeks strengthens memory far more than last-minute cramming. [[sources/learning-how-to-learn]]
- Do short, daily sessions on important topics, reworking problems or concepts the next day and then at increasing intervals to consolidate chunks. [[sources/learning-how-to-learn]]
- Spaced repetition forces the brain to retrieve and reconstruct knowledge repeatedly, strengthening neural pathways and preventing forgetting. [[sources/learning-how-to-learn]]
