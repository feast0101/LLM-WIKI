# Brain-Machine Interface

## Definition

A brain-machine interface (BMI) is a direct communication pathway between the brain and an external device, allowing neural signals to be read and decoded into commands or allowing external signals to stimulate neural tissue. BMIs typically work by recording electrical activity from neurons (either single units or population activity) and using algorithms to decode intended motor commands or other cognitive information.

## Why It Matters

BMIs represent the potential to restore function to individuals with neurological disabilities and more speculatively to augment human cognition by creating a direct interface with artificial intelligence. From a broader perspective, Neuralink frames BMIs as the next step in human-computer integration following language, writing, printing, and the internet—a direct brain-to-machine link.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Invasive BMIs**: Electrode arrays implanted directly into the brain, offering high spatial resolution and single-neuron recording capability.
- **Non-invasive BMIs**: Electroencephalography (EEG) or other surface-based methods, offering lower spatial resolution but no surgical risk.
- **Semi-invasive BMIs**: Electrodes placed on the brain surface but outside neural tissue.

## Evidence

- Current BMI research demonstrates that motor cortex neurons can be decoded to control computer cursors and robotic arms. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- Motor cortex contains unique neural codes for each individual—the specific pattern of electrical activity used to command "move arm left" differs from person to person, requiring individual calibration. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- The primary engineering challenge in BMIs is maintaining sufficient bandwidth and spatial resolution to decode complex intentions while remaining biocompatible for decades. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]

## Open Questions

- What is the minimum number of recorded neurons needed for high-fidelity motor control or cognitive interface?
- Can neural decoding models trained on one individual generalize to others, or must each BMI be individually calibrated?
- What is the maximum sustainable lifespan of implanted electrodes before immune response degrades signal quality?
- Can BMIs read high-level cognitive states (thoughts, intentions) or only motor commands?
