# Cortex

## Definition

The cortex (or cerebral cortex) is the outermost layer of the brain, approximately a nickel's thickness. It is composed primarily of gray matter and contains the cell bodies of neurons and their dendritic trees. The cortex is divided into specialized regions that handle different functions: the somatosensory cortex (sensation), motor cortex (movement), visual cortex (vision), and frontal lobe (higher cognition and executive function).

## Why It Matters

The cortex is where high-level cognitive functions occur, including thought, planning, and voluntary movement control. For brain-machine interfaces, the cortex (particularly the motor cortex) is a primary target for signal recording because it contains neurons that explicitly encode motor intentions.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Primary sensory cortices**: Direct recipients of sensory information (visual, auditory, somatosensory).
- **Primary motor cortex**: Issues commands to muscles via motor neurons.
- **Association cortices**: Integrate information from multiple sensory and motor regions.
- **Prefrontal cortex**: Involved in planning, decision-making, and higher cognition.

## Evidence

- The cortex contains approximately 15-20 billion of the brain's 100 billion neurons. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- The cortex is the command center of the brain, beaming orders out through white matter tracts beneath it. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- The visual cortex is one of the best-understood brain areas, with topographic mapping where neighboring cells represent neighboring regions of visual space. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- Motor cortex neurons encode movement commands, but each individual's neural code is unique; the patterns of electrical activity differ from person to person. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]

## Open Questions

- How are different functions distributed across the cortex, and can this mapping be predicted?
- How do cortical areas communicate to produce unified perception and action?
- Can high-level cognitive states (thoughts, intentions) be decoded from cortical activity?
