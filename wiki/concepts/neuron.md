# Neuron

## Definition

A neuron is a specialized cell in the nervous system that transmits electrical and chemical signals. It consists of a cell body (soma), multiple branching dendrites that receive signals from other neurons, and a long axon that transmits signals to other neurons or to muscles. Neurons are the fundamental computational units of the brain.

## Why It Matters

Understanding neurons is foundational to neuroscience and brain-machine interfaces. Neurons encode information through patterns of electrical firing (action potentials), and reading or stimulating these patterns is the basis for all current and proposed BMI technologies.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Interneurons**: Neurons that communicate with other neurons, found primarily in the brain and spinal cord. Most brain neurons are interneurons.
- **Sensory neurons**: Neurons that carry signals from sensory receptors in the periphery toward the central nervous system.
- **Motor neurons**: Neurons that carry signals from the central nervous system to muscles, causing contraction.

## Evidence

- The brain contains approximately 100 billion neurons, with 15-20 billion concentrated in the cortex. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- Neurons transmit information via action potentials—all-or-nothing electrical pulses that propagate along the axon. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- Neurons are binary information carriers: they either fire a full action potential or they do not; partial firing does not occur. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- Sensory and motor neurons can extend up to one meter in length, linking the spinal cord to the periphery. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
