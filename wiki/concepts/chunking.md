# Chunking

## Definition

Chunking is the process of compressing multiple pieces of information into a single, meaningful unit or pattern. A chunk is a compact mental pattern that groups many small ideas into one coherent whole, similar to how a well-practiced code pattern or chess opening serves as a single recognizable unit despite containing many individual elements.

## Why It Matters

Chunking transforms isolated facts into organized, retrievable knowledge. By grouping related information, chunking reduces the working memory load and allows the brain to operate at a higher level of abstraction. Expert knowledge in any domain is fundamentally built on well-developed chunks specific to that domain.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Concept-based chunks**: Understanding how ideas relate (top-down chunking).
- **Practice-based chunks**: Learning through repeated application until patterns become automatic (bottom-up chunking).
- **Hybrid chunking**: Combining understanding with varied practice to build robust, flexible chunks.

## Evidence

- Chunks are compact mental patterns that group many small ideas into one meaningful unit, like a well-practiced code pattern or math technique. [[sources/learning-how-to-learn]]
- To build chunks effectively: (1) focus on understanding a small concept, (2) practice it with varied examples, (3) revisit it over days so it becomes automatic and easy to recall. [[sources/learning-how-to-learn]]
- Building chunks requires spaced repetition and varied practice; one-time exposure does not create durable chunks. [[sources/learning-how-to-learn]]
