# Gray Matter

## Definition

Gray matter is the part of the brain composed primarily of neuron cell bodies (somas), dendrites, and synapses—the "processing" components of neural circuits. It appears darker in color due to the density of cell bodies. Gray matter is concentrated in the cortex (the brain's outer layer) and in deep structures like the limbic system and brainstem.

## Why It Matters

Gray matter performs the computational functions of the brain. Understanding where gray matter is concentrated and how it is organized provides insight into what functions each brain region performs.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Cortical gray matter**: The nickel-thick outer layer of the brain; contains 15-20 billion neurons.
- **Subcortical gray matter**: Deep brain structures like the limbic system and brainstem; contain the remaining ~85 billion neurons.

## Evidence

- Gray matter is made up of neuron cell bodies (somas) and their dendrites and axons, plus other supporting structures. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- Gray matter is darker in color than white matter due to the density of cell bodies. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- The cortex is a layer of gray matter containing approximately 15-20 billion neurons. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
