# Peripheral Nervous System

## Definition

The peripheral nervous system (PNS) consists of all neural tissue outside the central nervous system, including sensory and motor neurons that connect the CNS to the rest of the body. Sensory neurons bring information from the periphery to the spinal cord and brain; motor neurons carry commands from the brain to muscles.

## Why It Matters

The PNS is the interface between the central brain and the body. Understanding how sensory and motor information flows through the PNS is essential for brain-machine interfaces, which aim to replace or augment these natural communication pathways.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Somatic nervous system**: Sensory and motor neurons innervating skin and muscles (voluntary control).
- **Autonomic nervous system**: Neurons controlling internal organs (largely involuntary).

## Evidence

- The nervous system is divided into two parts: the central nervous system (brain and spinal cord) and the peripheral nervous system (neurons radiating outward from the spinal cord into the body). [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- Sensory and motor neurons of the peripheral nervous system can be up to a meter in length. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- Sensory neurons carry information from receptors in the body to the spinal cord and brain; motor neurons carry commands from the brain to muscles. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
