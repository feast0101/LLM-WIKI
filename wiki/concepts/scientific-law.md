# Scientific Law (Governing-Law Regime)

## Definition

One of four governing-law regimes in the agentic world modeling taxonomy. The Scientific Law regime encompasses systems where world models must respect causal structure, latent mechanisms, experimental observables, and the relationship between interventions and measurements. Domains include AI-driven scientific discovery, hypothesis-driven experimentation, surrogate modeling for experimentation, and mechanistic reasoning in domains like biology, chemistry, and physics research.

## Why It Matters

Scientific-law world models enable autonomous discovery and hypothesis testing. Unlike physical systems with deterministic dynamics or digital systems with explicit logic, scientific systems involve unobserved latent variables, stochastic mechanisms, and the need to infer causality from interventions. An effective scientific-law simulator must distinguish between correlation and causation, model measurement processes, and enable principled hypothesis testing.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Causal mechanism modeling**: Learn latent causal structures driving observed phenomena
- **Experimental surrogate models**: Predict outcomes of scientific experiments (synthesis, biological assays, physical measurements)
- **Hypothesis-driven simulation**: Generate predictions for specific hypotheses enabling hypothesis testing
- **Measurement process modeling**: Account for measurement noise and instrument-dependent observables
- **Mechanistic interpretation**: Prioritize interpretable, explainable models of underlying mechanisms
- **Active learning for discovery**: Design experiments to maximally reduce uncertainty about causal structures
