# Neurotransmitter

## Definition

Neurotransmitters are chemical molecules released from the axon terminal of a neuron into the synaptic cleft. They bind to receptors on the postsynaptic membrane, either exciting (depolarizing) or inhibiting (hyperpolarizing) the downstream neuron, affecting its probability of firing an action potential.

## Why It Matters

Neurotransmitters are the biochemical basis of neural communication. Different neurotransmitters have different effects, and the balance between excitatory and inhibitory neurotransmission shapes neural circuit behavior and plasticity.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Excitatory neurotransmitters**: Raise the charge of the postsynaptic neuron (depolarization), increasing likelihood of action potential.
- **Inhibitory neurotransmitters**: Lower the charge of the postsynaptic neuron (hyperpolarization), decreasing likelihood of action potential.

## Evidence

- Neurotransmitters are released from axon terminals and touch the dendrites (hair) of other neurons, either raising or lowering the postsynaptic neuron's charge. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- Whether a neurotransmitter causes an action potential in the downstream neuron depends on the cumulative effect of all inputs—if the charge exceeds threshold, firing occurs. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- Different neurotransmitter chemicals have different effects on the postsynaptic neuron. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
