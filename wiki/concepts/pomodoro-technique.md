# Pomodoro Technique

## Definition

The Pomodoro Technique is a time management method in which work is divided into focused intervals (typically 25 minutes) separated by short breaks. Each interval is called a "pomodoro." The core structure is: set a timer for 25 minutes, work with full focus and minimal distractions, take a short reward break (5 minutes), then repeat. After 4 pomodoros, take a longer break (15-30 minutes).

## Why It Matters

The Pomodoro Technique is particularly effective for combating procrastination because it shifts focus from the daunting end goal (the product) to a manageable, time-bounded work session (the process). This psychological reframing makes starting easier, and once started, the emotional resistance typically diminishes, allowing momentum to build.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Classic Pomodoro**: 25-minute work blocks with 5-minute breaks.
- **Extended Pomodoro**: 50-minute work blocks with 10-minute breaks (for tasks requiring deeper flow).
- **Short Pomodoro**: 15-minute work blocks for highly distractible individuals or short tasks.
- **Flexible breaks**: Adjusting break duration based on task difficulty and personal preference.

## Evidence

- Use the Pomodoro technique: turn off distractions, set a 25-minute timer, work with full focus, then take a short reward break. [[sources/learning-how-to-learn]]
- Focus on the process ("I'll work for 25 minutes") instead of the product ("I must finish this big task"), which makes starting easier and weakens procrastination habits over time. [[sources/learning-how-to-learn]]
- By creating fixed work periods followed by rewards, the Pomodoro technique leverages operant conditioning to build a habit of focused work. [[sources/learning-how-to-learn]]
