# Central Nervous System

## Definition

The central nervous system (CNS) consists of the brain and spinal cord. It is the primary computational and integrative center of the nervous system, receiving sensory information from the periphery and issuing motor commands through the peripheral nervous system.

## Why It Matters

The CNS is where all information processing occurs; understanding its organization and function is fundamental to neuroscience. For brain-machine interfaces, the CNS (particularly the cortex) is the target for signal recording.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Brain**: The upper part of the CNS; contains the cortex, limbic system, and brainstem.
- **Spinal cord**: The lower part of the CNS; a bundle of axons relaying information between the brain and peripheral nervous system.

## Evidence

- The nervous system is divided into two parts: the central nervous system (brain and spinal cord) and the peripheral nervous system. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- The spinal cord is a superhighway of axons carrying information from peripheral sensory neurons to the brain and motor commands from the brain to muscles. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
