# Action Potential

## Definition

An action potential is a rapid, temporary change in electrical potential across a neuronal membrane that propagates along the axon. It occurs when the membrane potential reaches a threshold, causing voltage-gated ion channels to open and allowing ions to flow across the membrane. The resulting electrical pulse travels along the axon at speeds ranging from 1 to 100 meters per second.

## Why It Matters

Action potentials are the electrical signals that carry information through the nervous system. Understanding how they are generated, propagated, and received is essential to understanding neural communication and how brain-machine interfaces can read or stimulate neural activity.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Propagation speed**: Varies from ~1 m/s in unmyelinated fibers to ~100 m/s in heavily myelinated fibers due to the effects of myelin sheath insulation.
- **Threshold dynamics**: The amount of neurotransmitter chemical stimulus needed to trigger an action potential varies based on the neuron's current state (facilitation and depression).

## Evidence

- Action potentials are binary events: either a neuron fires completely or not at all; there is no partial or variable amplitude. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- When an action potential reaches the axon terminals (feet), it causes neurotransmitter release into synapses, which may trigger action potentials in downstream neurons. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- The resting potential of a neuron axon is slightly negative; neurotransmitters can either raise or lower this charge, and if it exceeds threshold, an action potential is triggered. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
