# Motor Neuron

## Definition

A motor neuron is a type of neuron that transmits commands from the central nervous system to muscles, causing muscle contraction and movement. Motor neuron cell bodies are located in the motor cortex and brainstem, with long axons extending through the spinal cord and into the peripheral nerves that innervate muscles.

## Why It Matters

Motor neurons are the final efferent (outgoing) pathway of the nervous system and are where neural commands are translated into muscle action. For brain-machine interfaces, motor cortex neurons are a primary signal source because they explicitly encode movement intentions.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Upper motor neurons**: Located in motor cortex and brainstem; send commands down the spinal cord.
- **Lower motor neurons**: Located in the brainstem and spinal cord; directly innervate muscles.

## Evidence

- Motor neurons are one of two kinds of neurons in the peripheral nervous system; they carry commands from the central nervous system to muscles. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- Motor neurons can extend up to one meter in length from the motor cortex through the spinal cord to muscles. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- Motor cortex neurons encode movement commands; neurons in the motor cortex that connect to arm muscles fire when the arm moves, and different neurons code for different movement directions and forces. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- Each brain has a unique neural code for movement; the pattern of electrical activity in motor cortex is different for each individual, requiring individual calibration of BMI decoders. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
