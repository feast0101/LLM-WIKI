# White Matter

## Definition

White matter is the part of the brain composed primarily of myelinated axons bundled together in tracts. These axons carry signals between gray matter regions, connecting the cortex to lower brain structures and the spinal cord. White matter appears lighter in color because the myelin sheath wrapping the axons is fatty and white.

## Why It Matters

White matter forms the "wiring" of the brain, enabling communication between distant neural regions. Understanding white matter organization is important for understanding how different brain regions coordinate their activity.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Cortical white matter**: Axons connecting different cortical regions and carrying information from the cortex to lower brain structures.
- **Spinal cord white matter**: Axons carrying sensory information to the brain and motor commands to the body.

## Evidence

- White matter is composed primarily of axons carrying information between neurons or to destinations in the body. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- White matter is white because the axons are usually wrapped in myelin sheath, which is fatty white tissue. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- White matter sits beneath the cortical gray matter and contains axons of cortical neurons communicating with other brain regions and the body. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
