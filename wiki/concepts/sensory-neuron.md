# Sensory Neuron

## Definition

A sensory neuron is a type of neuron that transmits information from sensory receptors in the periphery (skin, muscles, organs) to the central nervous system. The cell body of a sensory neuron is located outside the spinal cord, and a single long axon extends from the sensory receptor all the way to the spinal cord, allowing the neuron to transmit information without relaying through multiple interneurons.

## Why It Matters

Sensory neurons provide the brain with information about the external world and the body's internal state. They are part of the afferent (incoming) pathway of the nervous system. Understanding how sensory neurons encode information is important for brain-machine interfaces that aim to provide sensory feedback.

## Variants / Approaches

- **Mechanoreceptors**: Respond to touch and pressure.
- **Proprioceptors**: Respond to body position and movement.
- **Nociceptors**: Respond to pain and temperature.

## Evidence

- Sensory neurons are one of two kinds of neurons in the peripheral nervous system (the other being motor neurons); they carry information from sensory receptors toward the central nervous system. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- Sensory neurons can extend up to one meter in length from the periphery to the spinal cord. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
- When a sensory stimulus (e.g., a fly landing on your arm) stimulates sensory nerves, the axon terminals start firing action potentials that carry the signal up to the spinal cord and brain. [[sources/neuralink-and-the-brains-magical-future]]
